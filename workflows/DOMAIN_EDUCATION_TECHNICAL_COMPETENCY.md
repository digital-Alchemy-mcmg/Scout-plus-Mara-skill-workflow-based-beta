# Education & Technical Competency — Evidence-Domain Extraction Contract

## Purpose

This module populates only the Education & Technical Competency evidence domain of Spatial Candidate DNA. It is upstream evidence placement, not résumé generation, target projection, or candidate evaluation.

## Reading direction

Begin from the Candidate Core and ask what this candidate can legitimately own in this domain. Then search the source for explicit evidence supporting that candidate-owned content. Do not begin by scanning for domain-looking phrases and attaching them back to the candidate.

## Controlling question

For every candidate statement ask:

> What credential, formal education, certification, technical skill, tool, system, or method does the source say this candidate holds, completed, or can demonstrably use — and does the source actually say enough to support that exact claim at that exact level?

If the specific credential/skill and the supported level of possession cannot both be named, do not place the item as established evidence.

## Admitted evidence

### Formal credentials

Degrees, diplomas, certifications, licenses, completed training programs, and formal coursework when the source names the credential, program, or issuing body. Preserve completion state exactly: earned/completed, enrolled/in progress, attended, discontinued, expired, or unresolved must not be collapsed into one another.

### Demonstrated technical competency

Named tools, languages, systems, platforms, methods, or technical skills that the source explicitly states the candidate used, applied, operated, configured, built with, or possessed proficiency in. Preserve the narrowest level actually supported.

Vague claims such as “tech-savvy” or “good with computers” do not qualify.

## Domain boundaries

### Creative Works & Projects

A source statement may support separate atoms in separate domains, but each domain takes only its own proposition. Example: “Built a database migration tool in Python” may support a Creative Works claim about the created tool and an Education & Technical Competency claim about stated Python use. The project’s impressiveness must not inflate the competency level.

### References / Publications / Testimony / Observed Behavior

Use portability as a boundary test for credentials. Portable personal qualifications such as ServSafe, OSHA 30, CPA, or Certified Scrum Master belong here when explicitly supported. Location-bound, period-bound, or team accolades such as Employee of the Month, Store of the Year, or accident-free awards do not become credentials.

### Work History

Role or duty titles are not certifications. “Lead Trainer” does not establish a certification. Explicit “Certified Trainer” language may establish one only to the level and issuer actually supported by the source.

## No inference or gap bridging

Do not infer a degree from a title, a certification from a role that normally requires one, mastery from tool proximity, completion from attendance, or ownership from participation. Preserve ambiguous evidence as ambiguous rather than silently upgrading or discarding it.

## Atomicity and provenance

Each placed item is a defensible atomic proposition with exact source provenance. Question-and-answer pairs that express one proposition produce one evidence item; the question and answer are not independent corroboration.

Every item preserves at minimum:

- original source evidence;
- extracted evidentiary atom;
- candidate relationship;
- credential/skill/system/method named;
- possession or completion level;
- evidence/source class;
- source lineage;
- extraction confidence;
- authority ceiling;
- ambiguity/corroboration state;
- placement rationale.

## Corroboration and duplication

Repeated wording from the same source lineage does not become independent evidence. Genuine separate mentions may corroborate only when they are distinct evidence objects and the source-lineage/independence rules support that relationship. Domain difference alone does not establish independence.

## Confidence and authority

Extraction confidence answers whether the source was read correctly. Authority ceiling answers how strongly that evidence class may support a candidate assertion. Keep them separate. Confidence tracks directness and completeness of evidence, not prestige, scale, or how impressive the underlying project appears.

Low or ambiguous evidence remains in the governed record when admissible; it is not silently erased merely because it cannot support a strong projection.

## Validation cases

A correct implementation must preserve these distinctions:

1. A question and answer expressing one competency produce one evidence item, not two corroborating mentions.
2. A portable exam-based certification is admitted when explicitly supported.
3. A location-bound accolade is excluded from this domain.
4. A deliberately ambiguous credential/accolade case remains flagged rather than guessed.
5. “Lead Trainer” without certification language does not become an invented certification.
6. Explicit “Certified Trainer” language is admitted only at the completion/issuer level actually supported.
7. Degree attendance and degree completion remain distinct.
8. A skills-list mention and a genuinely separate work-history statement of the same skill may corroborate when lineage and independence permit it.

## Empty-domain rule

If nothing qualifies, leaving this evidence domain empty is correct. Do not stretch, invent, or import evidence from another domain to populate it.
